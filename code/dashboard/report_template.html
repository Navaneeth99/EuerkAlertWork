Do press releases have an impact on a research paper?

We match 1.06 million research papers to EurekAlert! press releases and compare their attention scores, socialmedia coverage, and citations - controlling for where each paper was produced and published.

From the analysis, we see that within the same entity, press-released papers draw ~48× the usual attention and roughly 2× as many citations - a large, consistent association with press releases

The data

What

Research papers from universities, journals, and publishers. A small share get a press release on EurekAlert!; most don’t. We track what happens to each paper afterwards.

Why

Press offices spend real resources promoting research. Does promotion translate into measurable attention and scholarly impact - and how much?

How

Match papers to press releases by DOI, attach Altmetric mention counts and OpenAlex citations to every paper, and compare the two groups.

How we’ll measure impact

We never observe the same paper with and without a press release. Our strategy is to make the comparison as fair as possible by controlling for the characteristics of where a paper was produced and published, ending with models that compare papers only within the same journal or university.

Takeaway Only __HDR_PCT__ of papers get a press release, and outcomes are heavily skewed. A few papers get huge attention/citations; most get little or none. The averages get pulled around by that long tail - so a naive comparison of averages might be misleading

The models

What

A two-step ladder: a naive group comparison, then fixed-effects models that compare papers only within the same journal, university, field, or author.

Why

Some papers get more press releases and more attention because of journal/instituion prestige eg: Intelligent Computing journal have the highest percentage of press releases. Unless we hold the entity fixed, we credit the press release for what is really journal prestige.

Implications & conclusion

Press releases coincide with a large within-entity visibility premium-concentrated in Altmetric attention and news coverage, with a smaller but durable citation gain.

1 - Media attention

We see that press releases have an attention premium at +126 points on a without-PR baseline of ~2.7-roughly 48× the usual level for that entity. News mentions rise ~+14 from a baseline near 0.2, and X posts ~+46: for most outcomes the release is what moves a paper from invisible to widely discussed.
Overall the papers with press releases are having positive and significant effects on media attention for all controls

2 - Scholarly impact

Citations gain ~+58 within entity (~2× a without-PR mean of ~26). That premium survives pooled entity FE, university FE (+60) and last-author FE (+46), but falls to ~0 (not significant under 95% confidence) under journal-only FE.
In conclusion, the university papers with press releases are having positive and significant effects on citations although we are not seeing a positive effect on journal papers

3 - What’s next

These are associations from 1.05 million papers-only 1.3% matched to a press release-across 43 entities. They are strong and directionally consistent in every entity and outcome we plot, but they are not causal effects. Design that exploit release timing is the natural next step. Even as correlations, the attention and news premiums are large enough to inform how universities and journals invest in science communication.
